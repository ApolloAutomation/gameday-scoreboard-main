The F1 display shows Formula 1 all the time, on the board or in the [second area](/using/panel-sizes/#the-second-area), whatever team you follow. During a session it shows the running order. Between sessions it turns through cards: the next session and a countdown to it, the circuit's outline, the weather at the track, the drivers' and constructors' championships and the last race's result.

It's a separate display from following Formula 1 as your team, which [Racing](/using/racing/) describes. Your team, your favorites, their games and Home Assistant's **Game Status** carry on as before while the F1 display is up. It needs no account, sign-up or key.

The names, times and numbers in the examples on this page show the format, not real results.

## Turning it on

- **On the board:** on the device page, open **Showing** in the Panel card and pick **F1**. **Scoreboard** brings Game Day back.
- **In the second area:** on two rows of two, or three or four panels in a row, pick **F1** under **Shows** in the Quick settings card. See [The second area](/using/device-page/#the-second-area).

While the board shows F1, the second area's **Shows** list also offers **Game Day**, the scoreboard the board would show. When the board and the second area would both show F1, the second area shows the clock and says so.

Each change shows at once, with no restart. The panel only reads F1's data while F1 is on the panel.

## During a session

While a practice, qualifying, a sprint or the race is on track, the F1 display shows its running order and nothing else.

The top row is as on the [racing board](/using/racing/#during-a-session): "Lap 34/56" in green during the race, "FP3 live" or "Qual live" in practice and qualifying, "Red flag" in red. On a 128x64 display the race's name, like "US GP", is on the left of it.

Under it are the first six places:

| Column | 128x64 and wider | One 64x64 panel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place | In gray | In gray |
| Driver | Last name, up to 11 letters, in the team's color | Three-letter code in the team's color, like "LEC" |
| Pit stops or laps | In the race and the sprint, the pit stops so far. In practice and qualifying, the laps run | Not shown |
| Gap | To the leader, like "+13.07", or laps down, like "+1L"; "Leader" at the top. In practice and qualifying, the leader's lap time and everyone else's gap to it | After the code, like "LEC +13.07" |

The ticker leads with the race and the session's state, like "Race lap 34/56", with a red flag in red and a yellow flag in amber, then the top 10 with their full gaps, each driver's name in their team's color.

The codes come from the championship standings, so a driver whose code isn't the first three letters of their name gets their own.

## Between sessions

Between sessions the display turns through its cards, each for 10 seconds (the weather for 8):

1. **The latest session**, until a day after it started: its order, with the top row saying so, like "FP2 final" or "Final".
2. **Next session**: the race's name on the left and a countdown on the right, like "7d 20h", in amber with minutes and seconds, like "45:12", in its last hour. Under it are the weekend's sessions with their days and start times in the panel's timezone, like "Practice 1 Fri 12:30a", the next one in gold and the ones run in gray. On one 64x64 panel: "Fr" then "FP1 12:30a".
3. **Circuit**: the track's outline, with its length, laps, turns and lap record, like "Rec 1:33.36", and who set it and when, like "VER 2025". On one 64x64 panel the outline fills the panel, under the circuit's name.
4. **Weather at the track**: the sky, the air temperature, the chance of rain, the wind and any rain falling now.
5. **Drivers**: the top six with their points and, on 128x64, their wins, like "3W".
6. **Constructors**: the top six teams the same way.
7. **Last race**: its top six with the points they scored and their gaps, or "DNF".

A card with nothing to show yet is left out. Once the season's last race is over, the next session, the circuit and the weather drop out until the next race is scheduled, and the standings and the last race stay.

The ticker doesn't change from card to card, so it scrolls all the way through: the race weekend and its sessions to come, the weather at the track, the top three of each championship, the last race's podium and the circuit's facts, with drivers and teams in their colors.

## Units

With the panel's timezone in the US (Eastern, Central, Mountain, Arizona, Pacific, Alaska or Hawaii), temperatures are in °F, the wind in mph and circuit lengths in miles. In UK and Ireland the wind is in mph and circuit lengths in miles, with temperatures in °C. In any other timezone they're in °C, km/h and km. See [Timezone](/using/device-page/#timezone).

## Sizes and layouts

The F1 display fits wherever it goes: one 64x64 panel, a three-wide row's right panel, a 128x64 board or second area, and the whole of three or four panels in a row. On boards wider than 128, the rows stay 128 wide in the middle and the circuit's outline gets the room to their right. See [Panel sizes and layouts](/using/panel-sizes/).

## Night hours and Off or dim

In the [night hours](/using/brightness-and-clock/#night-hours) the clock takes the F1 display's place, on the board or in the second area, unless a session is on: from 10 minutes before a session's start until 90 minutes after it, or 3 hours after the race's, the F1 display stays up and keeps reading.

With the **Idle display** on **Off or dim** and F1 on the board, the panel goes dark or dim while no session is on, and lights up for each session.

## Where it gets its data

| What | From | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The running order | ESPN's race weekend summary | Every 30 seconds while a session is on track, every 5 minutes in the 2 hours before one and every minute in its last 10, every 30 minutes the rest of the season and every 6 hours between seasons |
| The schedule, both championships and the last race | Jolpica, an open Formula 1 data service | The schedule every 6 hours, every 30 minutes after the race starts. The standings and the last race every hour on a race weekend, every 15 minutes from 90 minutes to 8 hours after the race starts, and every 6 hours otherwise |
| The weather at the track | Open-Meteo | Every 15 minutes on a race weekend, every hour otherwise, and not at all between seasons |
| The circuit's facts and lap record | ESPN | Once for each circuit |

The circuit outlines come with the firmware, from the open [F1DB](https://github.com/f1db/f1db) database.

When a source keeps failing or turns the panel away, the display carries on without it:

- **Jolpica** failing three times in a row, or refusing the panel, sends the schedule to ESPN's own: the race weekend's sessions, or after the race the next weekend from ESPN's calendar. The standings stay as they were, with their age in amber in the top row, like "5h ago", and in the ticker, and so does the last race. The panel tries Jolpica again after half an hour, then after longer waits up to 6 hours, and goes back to it as soon as it answers.
- **Open-Meteo** failing the same way sends the weather to MET Norway's forecast, until Open-Meteo answers again on one of the same tries.
- **ESPN** failing leaves the order as it was and the panel tries again after a short wait, then longer ones. The other cards carry on.

Each source waits on its own, so one that's down never holds up the others. If nothing can be reached and the panel has nothing to show yet, the board says "F1" and the ticker "Can't reach the F1 schedule". With no race scheduled and no standings yet, as at the start of a new year, the ticker says "No F1 races scheduled".

## Not there yet

- Settings of its own, like picking which cards to show.
- Live timing beyond what ESPN's summary has: there are no sector times, tires or intervals between cars.
- A backup for the running order itself: when ESPN can't be reached, the order waits for it.
