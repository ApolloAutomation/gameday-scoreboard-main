Pick the players you follow, from any team in the football, baseball, basketball, hockey and soccer leagues the panel knows. While one of their games is on the board, the ticker shows their stat line whenever ESPN lists them among the game's leaders, and when they score, the celebration says their name, like **ALLEN TOUCHDOWN**. The panel keeps up to eight players. Golf tours and racing series have no players to pick, and neither do minor league baseball, AHL or ECHL teams: the rosters come from ESPN, which doesn't carry those leagues.

## Adding a player

1. Open the [device page](/using/device-page/) and find the **Favorite players** card.
2. Tap **Pick a team**. The team chooser opens, the same one you pick teams with, without the **Golf**, **Racing**, **MiLB**, **AHL** and **ECHL** tabs.
3. Your browser reads that team's roster from ESPN, and a **Player** list appears. Pick the player.
4. Tap **Add player**.

The card says **Added** once the panel has the player, and lists your players with their team. To take one off, tap the remove button (✕) next to them.

If the roster can't be read, the card says **Couldn't read the** team's **roster from ESPN** and asks you to check the internet connection of the phone or computer you're using: the roster comes straight from ESPN to your browser, not through the panel. The card also says so when a player is a favorite already, or when all eight are in use: **All 8 players are in use. Remove one first.** That includes a player added from another phone or computer a moment before, which the card may not have listed yet.

### The name on the panel

The panel shows a player's last name in capitals, like **ALLEN**. When a teammate has the same last name, the first initial comes first, like **J. HUGHES** and **L. HUGHES**, and when the initial is the same too, the first two or three letters of the first name, like **JE. SMITH** and **JA. SMITH**. Two teammates with the same first and last name get the same name on the panel, and the card lists them the same way.

A name is at most 10 letters, one line of a single 64x64 panel's celebration, so a longer one is cut short (**VALDES-SCA**). Letters the panel's font doesn't have become the nearest plain ones: accents come off, **Ø** is **O** and **Æ** is **AE**, so Martin Ødegaard is **ODEGAARD**.

If a player moves to another team, remove them and add them again from the new team's roster.

Your favorite players are a shortcut for [player props](/using/bets/#player-props) too. In the **Bets** card, with **Player prop** chosen, tap **Favorite players** and the **Player** list shows just your favorite players, from every team, so you can bet on one without picking their team first. Before adding the prop, your browser checks that the player is still on the team you added them from; if they've moved, the card says so, and you pick their new team instead.

## The stat line

While a favorite player's game is on the board and live, the ticker adds the player's line from ESPN's game leaders, like **ALLEN 21/34, 241 YDS, 2 TD**, after the game's own parts and before your [bets](/using/bets/). On two rows of two they run in the bottom half's ticker, the only one there. The device page shows the same lines as tags under its board. Each player has a line only while ESPN lists them among the leaders, so a player who isn't leading anything has none, and a line can come and go during the game.

What ESPN lists as leaders depends on the sport:

| Sport | Leaders | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Football | One passing, one rushing and one receiving leader for the whole game, from either team | **ALLEN 21/34, 241 YDS, 2 TD**, **COOK 19 CAR, 95 YDS** |
| Basketball | Each team's leader in points, rebounds and assists | **EDWARDS 19 PTS, 7 AST**, **GOBERT 9 REB** |
| Baseball | Each team's leading hitters | **JUDGE 1-3, HR, 2 RBI, R** |
| Hockey and soccer | None | No stat line |

A long line is cut after its last part that fits. There's no stat line before the start or after the final.

## The celebration

When a favorite player scores in the game on the board, the celebration says their name and the play, in place of the team's splash for that play, never as well. It gets the same colors and fireworks the team's splash would, whatever the player's name, and the same logo of the player's team with the points of the player's play, like **+6**, **+3** or **+2 RUNS**. They always match the play's words: a field goal shows **+3** even when a touchdown came in the same update, and **JUDGE 2 RBI** shows **+2** even when another run scored just before. A late lead change has no points. See [Splashes](/using/panel-sizes/#splashes).

| Sport | What names the player |
| --- | --- |
| Football | A touchdown, field goal or safety on a play ESPN credits to the player: **ALLEN TOUCHDOWN**, **BASS FIELD GOAL**. A touchdown pass is the receiver's. The try after a touchdown, an extra point or a 2-point conversion, never names a player, because ESPN credits it to the player who scored the touchdown, not the kicker: the team's **EXTRA POINT** or **2-PT** shows. When the touchdown and its try come between two reads, the touchdown names its scorer. |
| Baseball | A home run (**JUDGE HOME RUN**), a grand slam, runs on the player's hit (**JUDGE 2 RBI**), or a walk-off (**JUDGE WALK-OFF**). |
| Soccer | A goal, or a penalty goal (**SAKA PENALTY GOAL**). Never an own goal. |
| Hockey | A goal. ESPN doesn't say which player scored, only the play's words, like "Kyle Connor Goal", so the panel looks for the player's name there, among your favorites on the team that scored. It names a player only when exactly one of them matches. The initial helps: **J. HUGHES** and **L. HUGHES** are told apart, and a name cut short still matches (**NUGENT-HOP** is Ryan Nugent-Hopkins). Two favorites on one team with the same name and initial are not told apart, and then the team's splash shows. A teammate with your favorite's first and last name, who isn't a favorite, is named as your favorite: the words are the same. |
| Basketball | Only a late lead change (the last 2 minutes or overtime) or a buzzer beater, on the player's basket: **EDWARDS BUZZER BEATER**. Each time the panel reads the game, about every 10 seconds, ESPN gives only the latest play, so most baskets are never seen, and an ordinary basket doesn't celebrate. A buzzer beater names the player only when a read catches the basket as ESPN's latest play, before ESPN adds the end of the period; often it has, and then the team's splash shows. |

The panel compares each read of the game with the one before, so a score shows when the play that scored is the latest one ESPN lists at that read. If two plays come between reads, only the latest counts (a touchdown and its try are the exception above), and when ESPN posts the score before the play, the team's splash shows.

A play that ends the game, like a walk-off or a buzzer beater that wins it, names the player only when a read catches it while ESPN still has the game on. Once ESPN posts the final, it no longer lists the last play, so a game-ending play read only at the final gets the team's splash.

On one panel the name and the play go under the logo in two lines, like **ALLEN** over **TOUCHDOWN**, or in three in smaller letters when a long name comes with a two-word play. On a 128x64 display they share a line when they fit, or else take two lines in smaller letters, clear of the logo above them. On three or four panels in a row they sit between the logo and the points. On two rows of two the celebration covers all four panels, with the name and the play across the middle under the logo.

### Its switch

The **Celebrations** card on the device page has a **Favorite players** switch, **Scores**, with the switches for each sport. It starts on, on a new panel and on one that had its other switches set already.

- It works on its own: a favorite player's score is named even when the switch for that kind of play is off, and turning it off brings back the team's splash.
- **Opponent scores too** doesn't matter to it: a favorite on the other team is named too.
- **Favorite teams only** still applies in Live games: a game without your team or a favorite team on either side gets no splashes at all, a favorite player's included.

## Which games

Only the game on the board, in every mode. A favorite player whose team isn't on the board shows nothing, and in [Favorite teams](/using/favorites/) the favorites' games that aren't on the board are left out too. Follow the player's team, or make it a favorite, to see more of their games.

### Big plays in other games

When the board switches to a big play in another game (see [Big plays in other games](/using/modes/#big-plays-in-other-games)), that game is the one on the board for its stay. A favorite player in it gets their stat line, and when they score later in the stay, the celebration says their name.

The big play that brought the board there shows the team's splash, even when a favorite player made it, and only once. The panel's checks of other games see the score, not who scored, so they never name a player. For the same reason a favorite player never makes the panel switch: it watches and switches for teams only, and a favorite player's team counts as a favorite only if you made it one, for **Switch from my team** too. The **Favorite players** switch on its own doesn't make the panel check other games.

When the board goes back to its own game, a favorite player's score from while it was away doesn't celebrate late, just like the team's.

## Data and Home Assistant

The panel reads nothing more from ESPN for favorite players: no rosters and no extra pages. It keeps a little more of each read it already makes of a game, and only while a favorite player is in that game's league, so a panel without favorite players reads and keeps exactly what it did before. The rosters are read by your browser, once, when you pick a team in the card. A [player prop](/using/bets/#player-props) does read more: while a game with a prop is on, the panel reads that game's box score about once a minute. The stat line above stays the one from ESPN's leaders, whether or not a prop is on the game.

Home Assistant gets no new entities, and its **Game Status** leaves the stat lines out.

Favorite players are saved on the panel, so they stay through a restart or an update.
