Privacy

Effective 2026-11-12. This version adds Planespotters.net, where the panel and its web page get photos of the Flight Tracker's planes.

The short version

There's no account, no cloud service, and nothing tracking you. The app and the panel talk to each other over your home network, and the panel talks to ESPN for scores, to MLB for minor league baseball scores, to HockeyTech for AHL and ECHL scores, to GitHub for firmware updates, and, only while it shows one of its other displays or has Stocks & Crypto picked behind the night hours' clock, to Jolpica and a weather service for F1, to Yahoo Finance, Kraken and Nasdaq for Stocks & Crypto prices and to logo services for their logos, and to flight data services for the planes near the spot you pick for the Flight Tracker and to Google and FlightAware for those planes' airline logos and Planespotters.net for their photos. That's the whole picture.

What the panel does

The panel asks ESPN's public site for your team's schedule and scores, and downloads team logos from ESPN's image servers. For a minor league baseball team it asks MLB's public stats service (statsapi.mlb.com) instead, and downloads that team's logos from MLB's image server (midfield.mlbstatic.com). For an AHL or ECHL team it asks HockeyTech (lscluster.hockeytech.com), the stats service those leagues use for their own websites, sending the league's public site key as their sites do, and downloads that team's logos from HockeyTech's logo server (assets.leaguestat.com). While it shows the F1 display, it also asks ESPN for the race weekend and the circuit's facts (site.api.espn.com and sports.core.api.espn.com), Jolpica, an open Formula 1 data service (api.jolpi.ca), for the schedule and the standings, and Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com), or MET Norway (api.met.no) when Open-Meteo can't be reached, for the weather at the circuit, sending the circuit's location, never yours. While it shows Stocks & Crypto, it asks Yahoo Finance (query1.finance.yahoo.com) for the prices of the symbols you listed, and when Yahoo Finance can't be reached, Kraken (api.kraken.com) for coins and Nasdaq (api.nasdaq.com) for US stocks and funds. While the night hours' clock takes Stocks & Crypto's place, it still checks those prices every 10 minutes, unless you listed only coins. For the small logo at the start of each row, it downloads each stock's or fund's logo once from Parqet (assets.parqet.com), or, when Parqet has none, companiesmarketcap.com (companiesmarketcap.com) or Financial Modeling Prep (financialmodelingprep.com), and each coin's from Parqet, the open cryptocurrency-icons set on GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) or CoinCap (assets.coincap.io), sending only the symbol. It checks this site for firmware updates every six hours. None of that carries any information about you, only the team you picked, the symbols you listed and a request for the current schedule or prices.

If you turn on the Flight Tracker display, the panel keeps the home location you give it and, about every 20 seconds while the display is on screen, asks Flightradar24's public live map feed (data-cloud.flightradar24.com) for the planes in a box around it. If Flightradar24 doesn't answer, or the circle around your home crosses the 180th meridian, where Flightradar24's box can't reach, it asks adsb.fi (opendata.adsb.fi) or adsb.lol (api.adsb.lol) instead, sending the center of that spot rounded to about a kilometer and the distance you chose. For the airline flights it shows whose source has no full route (adsb.fi, adsb.lol, your own receiver and some Flightradar24 flights) it looks up the route from adsb.im (adsb.im), over plain HTTP, or from adsbdb (api.adsbdb.com) while adsb.im is failing, sending their callsigns and positions, not your location. If you give it the address of your own ADS-B receiver, it reads that on your network first. For the plane on its card, it downloads the airline's logo from Google Flights' logo images (www.gstatic.com) or from FlightAware (www.flightaware.com), whichever has the clearer one, sending only the airline's code. On a card 128 pixels wide or more, it also asks Planespotters.net (api.planespotters.net) for a photo of that plane and downloads it (t.plnspttrs.net), sending only the plane's address or registration. None of these needs an account or a key.

The panel's own web page, opened in your browser, asks ESPN for today's games, team logos and a team's roster when you add a favorite player or a player prop, MLB for today's minor league games and their logos, HockeyTech's logo server for AHL and ECHL logos, and GitHub for release notes, so those sites see your browser's address the way any website does. Nothing about you goes with it. When you type a town or a postal code for the Flight Tracker's home, the page sends it to Open-Meteo's place search (geocoding-api.open-meteo.com) to find its latitude and longitude; a latitude and longitude typed in goes straight to the panel. While the Flight Tracker has a plane on its card, the page asks Planespotters.net (api.planespotters.net) for a photo of it by the plane's address or registration, and shows that photo from t.plnspttrs.net.

What the app does

The app finds and talks to panels on your home Wi-Fi network only, using Bonjour to discover them and HTTP to read scores and send settings. First-time Wi-Fi setup happens over Bluetooth, directly between your phone and the panel. Your Wi-Fi password goes from the phone straight to the panel and is stored only on the panel. The app also fetches team logos from ESPN's image servers so you can see them while picking a team. The app has no server of its own, no account or sign-in, and does not collect analytics or usage data.

What we don't do

No accounts. No analytics or crash reporting. No advertising. The app and the panel collect nothing, so there's nothing to sell or share.

Like any website, ESPN, MLB, HockeyTech, GitHub (which hosts this site and the firmware updates) and, with the other displays, Jolpica, Open-Meteo, MET Norway, Yahoo Finance, Kraken, Nasdaq, Flightradar24, adsb.fi, adsb.lol, adsb.im, adsbdb, Google, FlightAware and Planespotters.net see the IP address a request comes from. We don't receive or keep any of that.
